Weather Radar vs Normal Radar

Same physics, opposite jobs. One wants a list, the other wants a picture.

On this page
01Targets versus fields 02The same wave 03Two pictures 04Worked example 05Eight differences 06Same system, different aim

Weather radar also sends and receives waves. How is it different from normal radar?

Normal radar looks mainly at hard targets like aircraft and ships. Weather radar looks mainly at precipitation particles like raindrops, hail, and snow. Weather radar cares more about echo strength, speed, and distribution. Think of the same flashlight — one shines at people, one shines at fog.

Weather radar often uses Doppler to measure wind speed. Dual-polarization weather radar can tell apart rain, snow, and hail. Weather radars work in networks to cover large areas.

Normal radar wants to find targets; weather radar wants to describe the weather.

01 TARGETS VERSUS FIELDS

A Dot or a Cloud

The single biggest difference is what counts as something worth seeing.

02 THE SAME WAVE

Identical Physics, Different Job

Both send X- or S-band pulses, bounce them off something, and listen. Everything after that diverges.

03 TWO PICTURES

What the Screen Actually Shows

One display answers "where is it". The other answers "how much, how fast, and what kind".

1. REFLECTIVITY -- where the water is, and how heavy

3. DOPPLER VELOCITY -- is it moving toward or away

5. DUAL POL -- is it rain, snow or hail

7. THE WARNING -- the picture becomes a polygon

Weather radar is really three radars in one housing. A flight radar is usually one, because one answer is enough.

04 WORKED EXAMPLE

Same Reflectivity, Different Meaning

The number on the scale does not say what fell. It says how much came back.

A ship returns 20 dBZ=alarming on a flight radar
20 dBZ of rain=light drizzle, below any warning threshold
A flock of birds=weak but very close, so very strong
Hail 5 cm across=very high dBZ and a polarimetric signature
Ground clutter=static, zero Doppler velocity
A tornado=adjacent reds and greens — that is rotation

This is why weather radar cannot stop at one product. A single number is ambiguous in a way an aircraft's echo never is.

05 EIGHT DIFFERENCES

The List Worth Knowing

Everything that makes a weather radar a different machine from an air traffic radar.

WHAT

Targets or Fields

A flight radar assumes a small number of discrete objects. A weather radar receives returns from everywhere inside its beam at once and must treat them as one continuous medium.

SCALE

Metres or Centimetres

Aircraft radar wants centimetre wavelengths for resolution. Weather radar uses decimetre wavelengths because longer waves penetrate rain instead of being smothered by it.

POWER

Kilowatts or Megawatts

A flight radar only needs to see aircraft with enormous radar cross sections. Hail is a poor reflector, so weather radar pushes hundreds of kilowatts to see rain at all.

RANGE

Kilometres or Hundred

Warning lead time is the point. A flight radar needs a few minutes; a weather radar needs an hour, so it sees ten times further and accepts far worse resolution.

DOPPLER

Optional or Essential

An aircraft's speed is found by tracking it between scans. Wind speed is only available from the Doppler shift inside a single pulse, so weather radar cannot work without it.

POLAR

Rare or Routine

Dual polarization tells a flight radar nothing extra about an aircraft. For rain it is the difference between rain, snow and hail — a routine requirement, not a feature.

SCAN

Fast or Slow

A flight radar spins quickly for a fresh picture of a fast-moving threat. A weather radar spins slowly because a storm changes more slowly than an aircraft does.

NET

Standalone or Networked

One air traffic radar guards one airfield. A storm crosses hundreds of kilometres, so national coverage only works as a network of stations sharing data.

Two of these are opposite: a flight radar trades resolution for speed, a weather radar trades resolution for range.

06 SAME SYSTEM, DIFFERENT AIM

Detect or Describe

The cleanest way to hold both in your head.

OUTPUT

A List or a Map

A flight radar produces a track file: identifiers, positions, velocities, intentions. A weather radar produces a grid of numbers covering every cell inside the beam.

ERRORS

Missed or Overcalled

A flight radar's failure mode is a missed aircraft, which is catastrophic and rare. A weather radar's failure is a false alarm, which is common and embarrassing.

ALARM

Per Target

Warnings attach to one aircraft and one interceptor. Weather warnings are polygons covering ground, because the danger is a region of wind and rain, not an object.

CLUTTER

Everything Else

For an aircraft radar birds, clouds and rain are noise to be filtered out. For a weather radar much of that noise is the signal, and ground clutter is the enemy instead.

HORIZON

Dome or Patch

Low-level flight radar covers a local cone. A weather radar sees the whole atmosphere out to the horizon, and is limited by curvature and beam height, not by the aircraft it serves.

MESSAGE

One Number or Many

A flight controller asks one question: is it safe to land? A forecaster asks six at once: how much, how fast, which direction, what type, how tall, and how long.

Same transmitter, same receiver, same mathematics. What differs is the question being asked of the result.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY

Weather radar watches precipitation; normal radar watches hard targets.
