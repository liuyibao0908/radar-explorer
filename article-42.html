The Servo System — How the Radar Turns

The neck. It carries a dish that can weigh thirty tonnes.

On this page

Many radar antennas spin all the time. How do they turn?

The servo system = motor + gears + control system. Its job: turn the antenna to a given direction at a given speed. Mechanical-scan radars use it to spin the antenna; phased-array radars don't spin the antenna, but some still turn their base.

Think of a camera gimbal that can pan and tilt. The servo must be steady and precise — if it shakes, the target is lost. It also has to track targets: when the target moves, the antenna follows.

Big antennas are heavy, so the servo needs enough power and a strong structure. If the servo fails, the radar "tilts its head."

01 THE THREE PARTS

Motor, Drive, Control

A motor alone would spin. Three things together make it point.

02 WHY INERTIA HURTS

A Thirty-Tonne Problem

The bigger the antenna, the harder it is to start, stop, and turn accurately.

Radar A dish=3 m airport antenna, ~2 t
Radar A slew=fast, can stop within a degree
Radar B dish=25 m weather antenna, ~30 t
Radar B slew=one full revolution takes minutes

Torque has to overcome inertia before anything moves, and braking has to absorb it after. Between those two limits sits the whole design problem.

03 ANIMATION: TRACKING

Watching the Antenna Follow

The target drifts. The servo has to catch up without overshooting.

1. STEADY -- the servo holds a fixed bearing

2. MOVE -- the target drifts and the command follows

3. LAG -- the heavy antenna trails behind

4. CORRECT -- the loop damps out the overshoot

Tracking is a control problem. Too little gain and the antenna lags; too much and it oscillates around the target.

04 WORKED EXAMPLE

How Fast Can It Turn?

Full revolution, degrees per second, and the beam width that makes it useful.

Sweep rate=6 rpm — one revolution in 10 s
Angular speed=360° in 10 s = 36°/s
Beam width=1° — time on one target
Illuminating a target=1° ÷ 36°/s = 28 ms per pulse
Pulses at 600 Hz=16 pulses hit one target
Integration needs 32=the scan rate drops to 3 rpm

Slew rate is not a free parameter. It is set by how many pulses you can afford to spend on one target before the whole sky has been swept once.

05 SPEED AND ACCURACY

Six Words Worth Knowing

The vocabulary of pointing a very large object at a small angle.

SLEW

Scan Rate

How many degrees per second the antenna can move. It sets the update rate for everything, because nothing is measured until the beam passes over it.

ACCURACY

Pointing Error

How far the beam actually points from where the computer believes. A tenth of a degree error is a kilometre of position error at long range.

WIND

Torque Compensation

Wind load on a large dish changes constantly. The controller measures it and biases the servo to hold the beam exactly where it was commanded.

BACKLASH

Gear Gap

A worn gear takes up slack one way and not the other, so the encoder disagrees with the command depending on direction. It is why precision drives are sealed.

DAMPING

Oscillation Control

A thirty-tonne mass on a bearing is a spring. Rate feedback is added so the loop damps the motion instead of ringing when it arrives.

ENCODER

Absolute Position

An optical encoder on the axis knows the bearing even after a power cut. Without it the antenna has to slew slowly home to find out where it is.

Every one of these exists because a dish is a large mass being asked to point at a precise angle.

06 WHY ARRAYS KEEP A SERVO

No Beam Rotation, Still a Motor

A phased array steers electronically. The pedestal still moves — just very slowly.

WHY

Steering Limits

A flat array can only steer away from its own face. Rolling the whole structure extends the reachable hemisphere and costs nothing electronically.

WHY

Sun and Ice

Over years the tower settles and ice accumulates. A slow continuous correction keeps the face true, and the encoder makes it verifiable.

TRACK

Storm Centroid

The servo is driven by where the significant weather actually is, not by a schedule. On a clear day it barely moves.

MODE

Sector Scan

Some arrays rotate slowly for a full-volume sweep and can instead park and dwell on a small sector when a storm needs continuous attention.

LOAD

Still Heavy

A 25 m radome on a tower is still tens of tonnes. Electronic steering removed the speed problem, not the structural one.

FAILURE

Tilt and Hold

If the servo fails on a fixed array, electronic steering usually stops too — a mispointed array is useless. Hydraulics park and hold the face as a last resort.

The phased array replaced the fast motor, not the motor. It just gets to be slow now.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY

The servo system is the radar's neck — it turns the antenna.
