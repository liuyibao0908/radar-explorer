The Signal Processor — The Radar's Brain

Noise is not the enemy. A fixed threshold is.

On this page
01The raw signal 02Four stages 03Doppler animation 04Worked example 05CFAR in detail 06Where the time goes

What the antenna receives is a mix — targets, clutter, and noise. How do you pick out the real target?

The signal processor's job: find real targets in the mess. Common methods include filtering, integration, Doppler processing, and constant false alarm rate detection.

Think of a teacher picking out one voice in a noisy classroom. Doppler processing separates moving targets from still clutter. Integration adds up multiple pulses to strengthen weak targets. Constant false alarm rate keeps alarms steady in different environments.

The faster it processes, the faster the radar reacts and the more targets it can track. Modern radars also use AI to help identify targets.

03 ANIMATION: DOPPLER SEPARATION

Moving or Standing Still

A still building and a moving aircraft look identical in range. Their Doppler is not.

1. RANGE -- both objects sit at the same distance

2. ACROSS PULSES -- the moving target shifts its phase each pulse

3. FFT -- the shift becomes a frequency, and shows up as a peak

4. SEPARATED -- clutter at zero speed, aircraft at its own

The FFT turns "how the phase changed" into "how fast". Zero Doppler means still, so buildings, ground and towers all pile up at the centre line.

04 WORKED EXAMPLE

Why the Threshold Cannot Be Fixed

Two radars, the same target, very different surroundings.

Radar A clutter=sea and sky — very low
Fixed threshold=works — few false alarms
Radar B clutter=forest and hills — very high
Same threshold=hundreds of thousands of false alarms
CFAR result=both report the same false-alarm rate

CFAR measures the local noise floor and sets the threshold just above it. The number of false alarms stays constant while the required detection threshold moves with the clutter.

05 CFAR IN DETAIL

Six Words, One Idea

Constant false alarm rate: the alphabet soup that hides the most important idea in detection.

CELL UNDER TEST

CUT

The one cell being decided. Its value is compared against the threshold — but never against the reference cells, so a real target cannot raise its own alarm level.

REFERENCE

Training Cells

A ring of guard-banded cells around the CUT, split into leading and trailing halves. They sample the local background without touching the target itself.

CA-CA

Cell Averaging

Take the mean of the reference cells and multiply by a factor alpha. Simple, but assumes the clutter is uniformly distributed — badly wrong next to buildings or sea clutter.

OS-CFAR

Cut the Outliers

Discard the largest few reference values before averaging, so one interfering target cannot drag the threshold up and hide a real one next to it.

CFAR

Clutter Estimate

Replace the mean with the median of the reference cells. Far more resistant to non-Gaussian clutter than any average, which is why it is the usual choice for real radars.

ALPHA

The Trade-off

Alpha sets how many false alarms you accept. Low alpha means few false alarms but missed weak targets; high alpha catches everything but floods the screen. There is no good value, only a chosen one.

Every CFAR variant is the same idea with a different statistic. The alphabet changes; the logic does not.

06 WHERE THE TIME GOES

The Arithmetic Budget

How much compute a single scan needs, and why the answer is always "more".

FFT

Most of the Work

One FFT per range cell per pulse, over 64 to 1024 pulses. A radar with 900 range cells and 64 pulses is doing about 58 000 transforms per dwell — every dwell, every scan.

GPGPU

Thousands of Cores

The transforms are embarrassingly parallel, so a radar signal processor is essentially a small supercomputer. Modern radars use FPGA plus GPU for exactly this workload.

GPU vs FPGA

Predictable or Flexible

FPGAs give fixed, guaranteed latency, which matters for a rotating antenna. GPUs are far more flexible and easier to reprogram, so most radars run both together.

RESOLUTION

The Cost of Sharpness

More pulses gives a finer Doppler resolution, and every extra pulse is another FFT. Doubling the pulse count doubles the compute for a factor of two in sharpness.

AI

What Machine Learning Adds

CFAR is optimal only for Gaussian noise. Real clutter is not, so neural networks trained on recorded data learn a decision CFAR cannot express — at the cost of a training set you have to keep current.

TRACKING

The Last Stage

After detection, a tracker such as a Kalman filter associates detections over time, predicts where each target will be, and assigns a track number. This is what makes the blip move smoothly instead of jumping.

Detection decides what exists now. Tracking decides what it is. The display only ever shows the second answer.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY

The signal processor is the radar's brain — it picks targets out of noise.
