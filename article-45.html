How Far Can Radar See?

There is no number. There is a budget, and everything spends from it.

On this page

Is farther always better for radar? How far can it actually see?

It depends on power, antenna size, and receiver sensitivity. More power, a bigger antenna, and a more sensitive receiver all mean longer range. The Earth's curvature also limits it: targets too low get hidden by the horizon.

Bigger targets with stronger reflections are easier to find. Think of shouting — your voice, the environment, and the listener's ears all affect how far you can be heard. Atmospheric refraction, rain, and fog also affect wave travel.

The radar equation can estimate range, but real conditions matter. So "how far" has no fixed answer — it depends on the situation.

03 ANIMATION: RANGE SCALES

Why Doubling Power Barely Helps

Four identical radars, four different power levels. Watch the range move.

1. 1 kW -- a short reach, a bird or a small boat

2. 10 kW -- ten times the power, only 1.8 times the range

3. 100 kW -- a long-range military or weather radar

4. 1 MW -- for a helicopter you would need about 1000 MW

This is the reason a radar is built the size it is. Power alone cannot buy range, so the aperture grows instead.

04 WORKED EXAMPLE

Two Targets at the Same Range

Same radar, same distance. One is seen, the other is not.

Radar=100 kW, 30 dB gain, 2 dB loss
Detection threshold=0.1 m² at 30 km
A gull=0.005 m² — missed at 30 km
A fighter aircraft=5 m² — seen well past 300 km
A stealth shape=0.01 m² front-on — missed at 30 km
Same ship, beam aspect=10 000 m² — seen at 600 km

Detection range scales as the fourth root of cross section. Multiply the target area by ten thousand and you only quadruple the range.

06 WHAT THE AIR DOES

The Valley Is Not Empty

Between radar and target there is always air, water and sometimes a storm.

RAIN

Attenuation

Rain scatters and absorbs. At X-band in heavy rain, loss grows so steeply with range that a short-wavelength radar can be blinded at 30 km by a storm it is looking through.

WAVELENGTH

The Rain Problem

Longer wavelengths suffer less, which is why weather radars for the 10 cm band see through storms that stop an X-band airborne radar entirely.

FOG

Fog and Cloud

Radar was invented partly to see through weather. Fog has almost no effect on radio waves, which is exactly why radar penetrates it and light does not.

BEND

Refraction

Radio waves bend slightly downward through the atmosphere, extending the radio horizon beyond the geometric one. Temperature inversions can extend it far enough to see ships beyond the horizon.

CLUTTER

Sea and Land Clutter

Over the sea, ground clutter stops close to the radar, so a ship near the horizon may still be seen above it. Over land, buildings clutter the near range and hide everything low.

ASPECT

Which Side You See

A ship seen broadside has an enormous cross section. The same ship seen bow-on can be a hundred times smaller. Aspect alone can double or halve the detection range.

This is why a published range figure is a best case: one radar, one target, clear air, beam aspect.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY

Radar range depends on power, sensitivity, and the Earth's curvature.
